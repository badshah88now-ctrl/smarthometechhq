Smart Home for New Homeowners: The First 5 Devices to Buy

By SmartHomeTechHQ

Moving into a new home is the best time to start a smart home, because you get to choose the setup instead of working around someone else's. But the temptation is to buy everything at once. Don't. Buy five devices, in this order, and you will have a home that is safer, cheaper to run and easier to live in — without a single gadget gathering dust in a drawer.

First, a video doorbell. It is the device you will use every single day, and it answers the question every new homeowner asks most: who is at the door? Second, a smart lock on the front door, so you can lock up from bed, let a friend in while you are out, and never hide a spare key under a mat again. Third, a smart thermostat — a new home means a new heating and cooling pattern to learn, and a thermostat that learns your schedule pays for itself by not heating an empty house. Fourth, two or three smart bulbs in the rooms you use most, on a schedule, so the house never looks empty when you are out. Fifth, a smart plug on the biggest energy waster you own — usually the old TV setup or a space heater — so you can kill its standby power with one tap.

Before you buy any of it, pick one platform and stick to it. Alexa, Google Home and Apple HomeKit all work well, but mixing them means three apps, three routines and three things to go wrong. Check that every device you buy works with the platform you picked, and prefer devices that work over Wi-Fi or the Matter standard so they keep working even if you change your hub later. And buy for the house you actually have: a small flat needs one hub and a handful of devices, while a three-storey house needs a mesh Wi-Fi network first, before any smart device will work reliably.

Keep the budget honest. A good starter setup costs less than a single month of takeaways if you buy the five devices above and stop there for a while. Live with them for a month, notice what actually annoys you, and let that decide what comes next.
