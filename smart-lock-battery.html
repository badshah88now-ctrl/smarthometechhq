Smart Locks: What Happens When the Battery Dies (and How to Avoid Lockouts)

The most common worry about a smart lock is simple: what if the battery dies and I am locked out? In practice, lock makers have thought about this, and a little preparation removes the risk almost entirely.

First, the warning signs. Most smart locks warn you for weeks before the battery runs out — a low-battery alert in the app, a flashing light on the lock, sometimes a different beep when it locks. Do not ignore the first alert; swapping four AA batteries takes two minutes and costs very little.

Second, the backups. Many locks keep a physical key slot, so your ordinary key still works even with a dead battery. Others have a small port on the outside where a 9V battery gives the lock just enough power for one unlock. Check which backup your model has before you need it, and keep a spare key with someone you trust, just as you would with any lock.

Third, the habits that prevent the problem: set a calendar reminder to check battery level every few months, use good-quality batteries rather than the cheapest pack, and avoid extreme cold on the lock side of the door where you can. Finally, test the backup once, on a calm afternoon, before you ever need it. Unlock with the key, or hold a 9V battery to the port and enter your code, so the steps feel familiar. A two-minute test now is far better than learning the process in the rain at midnight.
