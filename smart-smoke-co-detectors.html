Smart Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Detectors: Worth It?

A smoke alarm does one job, and it does it well — but only if someone is home to hear it. A smart smoke and carbon monoxide detector does the same job and adds two things an ordinary alarm cannot: it tells your phone when it goes off, and it tells you which room it is coming from. For a household that is often out, or for a home with bedrooms on more than one floor, that difference matters.

The phone alert is the feature that justifies the price. A kitchen fire that starts while you are at work is a disaster if nobody knows for hours, and a small problem if you find out in the first minute and call a neighbour. The same applies to carbon monoxide, which has no smell and no sound of its own — an alert on your phone while you are away could be the warning that an ordinary alarm in an empty house never delivers.

Smart detectors also cut down on the most annoying part of ordinary alarms: the 3 a.m. low-battery chirp. Most smart models send a phone notification days before the battery runs low, and some speak a plain message — "smoke in the kitchen" — instead of a siren you have to locate by ear. If several detectors are linked, they can all sound together so the whole house hears the alarm at once.

Placement follows the same rules as ordinary alarms. Put one inside each bedroom, one outside each sleeping area, and one on every level including the basement. In the kitchen, mount it away from the cooker so burnt toast does not set it off every week. Carbon monoxide detection belongs near sleeping areas and near any fuel-burning appliance, such as a boiler or a gas heater.

Before buying, check two things. First, whether the detector needs a hub or connects straight to Wi-Fi — a hub adds cost but usually means a longer battery life. Second, whether it is certified to the safety standard for your country, such as UL in the United States. A smart detector that is not properly certified is worse than an ordinary one that is, because it invites trust it has not earned.

Smart detectors are not a replacement for the rest of home safety. They work alongside the devices that protect the home while you are in it — a guide to the best smart home devices for beginners covers the starter set, and a home security camera buying guide covers what to watch once an alert tells you something is wrong.

If the budget only stretches to one or two, start with the hallway outside the bedrooms and the level where the boiler lives. Those are the two places where an early warning is worth the most, day or night.
